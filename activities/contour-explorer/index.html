From the side · cross-section

Contour interval means the difference in elevation between neighboring contour lines. With the map scale and contour interval unchanged, closer lines mean a steeper slope.

Try it: Are A and B equally high?

Yes. They are on the same contour line, so they have the same elevation—even though they are on opposite sides of the hill.

Try it: Change the interval from 100 to 200 feet. Did the mountain grow?

No. The summit is still 1,000 feet. We draw fewer lines because each step in elevation is now larger. Wider line spacing alone does not prove a gentler slope if the contour interval changed.

Try it: Make the hill narrower. What happens?

The same rise happens over a shorter horizontal distance. The sides become steeper and the contours move closer together.
